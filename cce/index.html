Two limits, two contexts

Public water: 1,4-dioxane 1 ppb; PFOA and PFOS 10 ppt each. Advanced oxidation can remove dioxane. Products: household cleansing and personal-care products moved from 2 ppm in 2022 to 1 ppm in 2023; cosmetics have a separate 10 ppm limit. Clean water is a systems problem.

Policy, household bills, maps, transparency, treatment, and infrastructure are connected. The same evidence that made contamination visible also created obligations and costs.

New York policy timeline

2017

CCE report + map: Annual reports from 58 public water suppliers made widespread dioxane contamination and potential sources visible.

2018-19

Product testing: An independent lab tested 30 products, then 80; the Dirty Dozen, Green Fifteen, and product-law campaign followed.

2019-26

Product rule: Signed December 9, 2019; household cleansing and personal-care products moved from 2,000 ppb in 2022 to 1,000 ppb in 2023. Cosmetics have a separate 10,000 ppb limit. NYSDEC finalized implementing regulations in September 2024; waiver-free compliance began January 1, 2026.

2020

Drinking-water standard: New York set 1 ppb for dioxane and 10 ppt each for PFOA and PFOS; advanced oxidation offered a treatment path.

2021

Transparency check: CCE examined supplier deferrals, resident letters, PFAS, dioxane, and impacted districts.

2022-26

Investment + follow-up: The Bond Act, Yale tap-water and voluntary blood-sample study, 2024 detergent retest, and 2025 map using 2024 data tracked implementation.

Other policy layers: PFAS food packaging, bag waste, polystyrene, apparel, and proposed cosmetics controls address different sources; they are not substitutes for dioxane controls.

04 / Laws

New York policy timeline

Key state and local measures affecting drinking water, products, waste, and infrastructure.

Enacted lawProposed billCCE connection
Policy timeline
2019–26
LAW · CCE VICTORY

Senate Bill S4389B: 1,4-dioxane product limits

Signed by the Governor in 2019, the law set category-specific limits. Household cleansing and personal-care products were limited to 2,000 ppb on or after December 31, 2022 and 1,000 ppb on or after December 31, 2023. Cosmetics have a separate 10,000 ppb limit from December 31, 2022. NYSDEC finalized implementing regulations in September 2024; approved waivers could not remain in effect after December 30, 2025, so waiver-free compliance began January 1, 2026.

Read the bill ↗NYSDEC rules ↗
2020
LAW · PFAS SOURCE CONTROL

S8817 / A4739-C: PFAS in food packaging

New York banned intentionally added PFAS in certain direct-contact food packaging. It took effect December 31, 2022.

Read the bill ↗
2020
LAW · STATEWIDE WASTE REDUCTION

New York State Bag Waste Reduction Act

Plastic products and packaging are part of the broader chemical-source and waste pathway: PFAS, including PFOS and PFOA, have been used in some treated materials and food packaging, while 1,4-dioxane is associated with certain product ingredients and manufacturing. Reducing plastic carryout-bag waste therefore supports pollution prevention and water-quality goals indirectly, but this law does not set limits for those chemicals.

Testing shows reformulation can work

NYSDEC adopted final 1,4-dioxane product regulations in September 2024. CCE's January 2024 follow-up testing showed major reductions in five re-tested detergents. CCE's 2026 case study calls 2025 the implementation milestone; the statutory waiver endpoint means all products had to comply beginning January 1, 2026.

Treatment is possible, but it is not free.

What this does not mean

Product reformulation can reduce new product-related contamination; it does not erase historic groundwater plumes or eliminate PFAS. Water suppliers still need monitoring, treatment, and infrastructure funding.

Industry spillover

CCE also reports that Procter & Gamble reduced 1,4-dioxane in all products sold in North America after New York's law. That is a CCE-reported outcome, not an independent market-wide audit.

Glossary

Key terms

A quick reference for the terms used in the reports and maps.
Data note: Map names and report publication dates do not always match the underlying water-quality data year. Health information is presented for public education and is not medical advice.

1,4-Dioxane

A manufacturing byproduct associated with some household and personal-care products.

PFAS

A large family of man-made chemicals known for persistence and resistance to heat, water, and oil.

PFOS / PFOA

Two specific PFAS chemicals monitored in public drinking water.

MCL

Maximum contaminant level: an enforceable public-water standard, not a product concentration or personal dose.

Waiver / deferral

A temporary regulatory allowance or delayed compliance status; it does not mean the underlying contaminant is safe.

ppb / ppt

Parts per billion and parts per trillion, units used to describe concentration.
